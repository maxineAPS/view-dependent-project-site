How to use it

  1. Views. Each card is one viewing direction (azimuth and elevation, in degrees) and the picture seen from it. Click a picture to upload your own (PNG or JPEG), or pick a built-in target. Two to four views work; views closer than about 40° apart compete for the same bar faces.
  2. Step 1: Optimize. Runs grad_descent() live: annealing, coarse to fine subdivision, alternating colour and height updates. The chart shows the loss of each view.
  3. Step 2: Project & make printable. Projects each picture onto the vertical segments of the bars its camera sees (as in the paper), blends overlapping views, slices the surface thin and builds a closed textured mesh.
  4. Look from view on a card shows the surface along that camera direction. Download gives the mesh (OBJ, MTL and texture) plus the heights, colours and cameras as files the repository’s reproject.py reads.

Everything runs in your browser and images are not uploaded. We ported the code release from Python and PyTorch to JavaScript for this page. The method is explained in the walkthrough.
